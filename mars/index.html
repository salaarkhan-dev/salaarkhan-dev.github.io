A LETTER TO MARIA

“Composed with care, and sent with every good wish.”

Break the seal.

The Eighth of August, Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

My dearest Mars,

I have called you Mars for so long that I have to reach for your real name. It appears on the envelope only because the postmaster insisted upon something official. To me, you have always been Mars.

There are places that quietly become part of a memory. Whenever I think of Islamabad, I cannot help but remember those evenings wandering through F-6 and F-7, arguing over which café deserved another visit, taking the longer route home simply because neither of us wanted to say goodbye. Those streets have long since returned to being just streets, yet they remain among my happiest memories.

You had a name for me, too. Salaari. Nobody else ever has, and I have decided nobody else ever will.

Since I am admitting things: I miss you. The way you laugh, and the way you smile. Your stickers on WhatsApp, of all things. I did not expect those to be what I would miss, and they are. Sitting down to something good with you. Gossiping about our families late into the night.

But today is not for looking backwards. Twenty-six, and a Saturday, which is the correct arrangement for a birthday: a day with nothing else in it. I hope you meet with every kindness this year, and with many quiet reasons to smile. I hope you still laugh the way you used to laugh with me.

Happy birthday, Mars.

I remain, as I have always been, affectionately yours —

P.S. — There is more written on this page than you can presently see. The room is too bright for it. Find the little lamp in the corner, switch it on, and take the light slowly down the page.

The lamp is lit. Move it down the margins.
